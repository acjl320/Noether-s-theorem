# Teorema de Noether · Noether's Theorem

** Author:** Jhemerson Alvarado
· [![ORCID](https://img.shields.io/badge/ORCID-0009--0004--8141--3241-a6ce39?logo=orcid&logoColor=white)](https://orcid.org/0009-0004-8141-3241)
**  Institution:** Universidad Nacional Mayor de San Marcos, Lima – Perú
**  Date:**  · February 2024

 ---
 
  This work derives Noether's Theorem from the Principle of Stationary Action, showing how every continuous symmetry of the action implies a conserved quantity.

  Starting point. For a system with generalized coordinates $q=(q_1,\dots,q_n)$ and Lagrangian $L=K(\dot q)-V(q)$, the action is defined as the functional

  $$S[q]=\int_{t_1}^{t_2} L(q,\dot q,t),dt.$$

  The principle of stationary action ($\delta S = 0$) leads to the Euler–Lagrange equations:

  $$\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot q_i}-\frac{\partial L}{\partial q_i}=0.$$

  Symmetry transformations. An infinitesimal transformation (parameter $\epsilon$) is considered, acting on both the trajectory and time:

  $$q_i' = q_i + \epsilon,K_i(q,\dot q,t), \qquad t' = t + \epsilon,\theta(q,\dot q,t).$$

  It is a Noether symmetry if the action stays invariant to first order in $\epsilon$ (up to an additive constant).

  Result (conservation law). When the transformation above is a symmetry, the quantity

  $$I=\sum_i p_i K_i - E,\theta + f, \qquad p_i=\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot q_i}, \qquad E=\sum_i p_i \dot q_i - L,$$

  is conserved along the physical trajectories of the system. Special cases: invariance under time translations ($\theta=1,\ K_i=0$) yields conservation of energy $E$; invariance under
  spatial translations yields conservation of momentum $p_i$.

  Example — Momentum conservation in an isolated particle system. For $N$ isolated massive particles, the total momentum is a constant of motion:

  $$\sum_{i=1}^{N} \mathbf{p}_i(t) = \mathbf{p} = \text{const.}$$

  Hence the trajectory of one particle is fixed by the others:

  $$\mathbf{p}1(t) = \mathbf{p} - \sum{i=2}^{N}\mathbf{p}_i(t),$$

  so that conservation of total momentum "bends" the path of particle 1, suggesting an effective gravitational field generated by the remaining particles.
